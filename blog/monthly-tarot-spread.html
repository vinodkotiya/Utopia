A Monthly Tarot Spread to Start Each Month With Clarity

By Utopia Wellness & Gifts · August 16, 2026

A monthly tarot spread is a simple, intentional way to check in with yourself at the start of each new month. Rather than pulling a single card and hoping for the best, a dedicated monthly layout gives you a fuller picture — what energy is arriving, what needs releasing, and where your growth edges are. It's like sitting down with a wise friend who sees the bigger picture and gently points you toward what matters most in the weeks ahead.

Whether you're new to tarot or you've been reading for years, a monthly spread creates a rhythm of reflection and intention that deepens your relationship with the cards over time. It gives you something to return to — a guidepost you can revisit as the month unfolds and circumstances shift. In this guide, we'll walk you through a powerful 6-card monthly spread, how to pair it with new moon energy, and tips for journaling your insights so nothing gets lost.

Why a Monthly Tarot Spread Is Worth Your Time

Daily card pulls are wonderful for quick check-ins, but they can sometimes feel scattered — one day's message doesn't always connect to the next. A monthly spread gives you a wider lens. It captures the arc of an entire cycle: where you've been, where you're heading, and what unseen forces are at play beneath the surface.

This practice works on two levels. First, it invites reflection — pausing before you rush into a new month to acknowledge what the previous one taught you. Second, it supports intention-setting — giving you a clear sense of what to focus on, what to protect, and what to release. When you combine these two elements, your tarot practice becomes less about prediction and more about conscious living.

Many of the readers at Utopia Wellness & Gifts on Lonsdale Ave in North Vancouver use monthly spreads as part of their own practice and recommend them to clients who want to build a more consistent, empowering relationship with tarot.

The 6-Card Monthly Tarot Spread Layout

This spread uses six cards arranged in two rows of three. It's designed to give you a complete snapshot of the month's energy without overwhelming you with too many positions. Shuffle your deck while focusing on the month ahead, then lay out your cards in this order:

  • Card 1 — Theme of the Month — This card reveals the overarching energy or lesson that will colour your entire month. Think of it as the soundtrack playing in the background of everything you do.
  • Card 2 — What to Release — What habit, belief, fear, or attachment is ready to be let go? This card shows you what's weighing you down or no longer serving your growth.
  • Card 3 — What to Embrace — The quality, opportunity, or energy you're being invited to welcome in. This is what wants to grow when you make space for it.
  • Card 4 — Challenge Ahead — A heads-up about a potential obstacle or area of friction. This isn't meant to scare you — it's meant to prepare you so you can navigate it with grace.
  • Card 5 — Hidden Support — The unseen help available to you this month. This could be a person, a resource, an inner strength, or spiritual guidance you haven't noticed yet.
  • Card 6 — Outcome and Advice — Where the month is likely to lead if you honour the guidance of the previous five cards. It often doubles as a piece of advice for how to approach the weeks ahead.

Take your time with each position. Read the cards individually first, then look at how they speak to each other. The relationship between Card 2 (release) and Card 3 (embrace) often tells a particularly powerful story about your personal evolution this month.

How to Use This Spread with New Moon Energy

While you can pull this spread on the first of any month, there's something especially potent about aligning it with the new moon. In many spiritual traditions, the new moon represents fresh beginnings, planting seeds, and setting intentions in the darkness before they grow toward the light. It's the natural starting point of a new cycle.

To pair your monthly spread with new moon energy, try this simple ritual: On the evening of the new moon, clear your space with sage or palo santo. Light a candle. Take a few deep breaths and release the previous month — mentally set it down. Then shuffle your deck with the question: "What does this new lunar cycle hold for me?" Lay your six cards and spend at least 15 minutes sitting with the messages before writing anything down.

The new moon's quiet energy makes it easier to hear subtle messages from the cards. You're not competing with the busy, outward energy of a full moon — instead, you're turning inward, which is exactly what a monthly check-in requires. If the new moon falls mid-month, you can still use it as your personal "month reset" and let the lunar cycle guide your timing instead of the calendar.

Journaling Tips for Your Monthly Reading

A reading without notes is a reading you'll likely forget within a week. Journaling transforms a one-time card pull into a living document you can learn from over months and years. Here's how to make the most of it:

  • Record the basics — Write down the date, moon phase, and each card in its position. Include whether cards appeared upright or reversed.
  • Capture your first impression — Before reaching for any guidebook, write what you feel each card is saying. Your intuition often picks up what textbook definitions miss.
  • Note emotional responses — Did any card make you uncomfortable? Excited? Confused? These reactions are data. They point to where the real growth work lives.
  • Write one intention — Based on the full spread, distill a single intention or mantra for the month. Keep it somewhere visible — your mirror, phone wallpaper, or altar.

Over time, your tarot journal becomes an incredible record of your personal growth. You'll start to notice patterns — recurring cards, themes that span multiple months, and how your interpretive ability deepens the more consistently you show up for this practice.

When to Revisit Your Monthly Spread

Your monthly reading isn't a one-and-done event. The cards often reveal deeper layers as the month progresses and you gain more context. There are a few natural moments to come back to your spread:

  • Mid-month check-in — Around the full moon (roughly two weeks in), revisit your cards. Has the "Challenge Ahead" card shown up yet? Are you honouring what Card 3 asked you to embrace?
  • When you feel lost or stuck — If you hit a difficult week, return to your spread. The "Hidden Support" card especially may offer clarity you weren't ready to see at the start of the month.
  • End-of-month reflection — Before pulling your next monthly spread, look back at the previous one. What played out as expected? What surprised you? This reflection is where real tarot wisdom is built.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I do a monthly tarot spread?

Once per month is ideal — either on the first of the month or aligned with the new moon. Consistency matters more than frequency. Pulling a monthly spread at the same time each cycle helps you build a reliable rhythm and makes it easier to track patterns across months. Avoid pulling a new monthly spread mid-month just because you didn't like what the first one said.

Can beginners use this 6-card monthly spread?

Absolutely. This spread is designed to be accessible for readers at every level. Each position has a clear focus, so you don't need to juggle complex relationships between cards right away. Start by reading each card on its own, then gradually notice how they connect. If you're just starting out and want hands-on support, the readers at Utopia Wellness & Gifts in North Vancouver can walk you through your first monthly spread in person.

Do I need to use a specific tarot deck for monthly readings?

No — use whichever deck you feel most connected to. Some people like to dedicate one deck to monthly readings and another for daily pulls, but this is a personal preference rather than a rule. The most important thing is that you feel comfortable with the imagery and that the deck feels responsive in your hands. If you're looking for a new deck, visit us at 1826 Lonsdale Ave to explore our curated selection.

What if I pull a scary or negative card in my monthly spread?

No card in tarot is purely negative — even the Tower or the Ten of Swords carry messages of necessary change and eventual healing. If a challenging card appears, look at its position in the spread for context. A difficult card in the "Challenge Ahead" position is simply preparing you, while the same card in "What to Release" may confirm that you already know something needs to end.
