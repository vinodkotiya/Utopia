A Poetic Guide to the 22 Major Arcana Tarot Cards

By Utopia Wellness & Gifts · July 12, 2026

The 22 Major Arcana cards form the soul's journey through tarot — a sweeping arc from innocence to wholeness, from the leap of The Fool to the completion of The World. Each card holds a universe of meaning, but sometimes the quickest way to feel a card's essence isn't through textbook definitions. It's through poetry. A few carefully chosen words can unlock understanding that pages of explanation cannot.

Below, we offer a poetic line or two for each of the 22 Major Arcana — small invocations to help you remember their energy, their warnings, and their gifts. Whether you're a beginner learning the cards or a seasoned reader seeking fresh perspective, let these verses serve as touchstones. The 22 Major Arcana in Verse

Let each card speak in its own voice. Read these slowly — perhaps aloud — and notice which lines land in your body. That resonance is wisdom speaking.

  • 0 — The Fool: Step off the edge with open hands; the net appears mid-flight.
  • I — The Magician: All tools laid before you — now channel will through fingertips to earth.
  • II — The High Priestess: She sits between two pillars, holding what silence knows and words cannot carry.
  • III — The Empress: The world blooms where she walks — abundance rooted in fierce, patient love.
  • IV — The Emperor: Stone throne, steady hand; structure is not a cage but the bones that let a body stand.
  • V — The Hierophant: Ancient keys passed down through open doors — tradition as a living river, not a locked room.
  • VI — The Lovers: Two become one choice; the heart commits and in committing, becomes whole.
  • VII — The Chariot: Opposing forces held in reins of will — victory belongs to those who move despite the pull.
  • VIII — Strength: The lion rests beneath a gentle hand; true power whispers, never demands.
  • IX — The Hermit: A lantern raised on a mountain path — the answers live in the quiet after you stop asking.
  • X — Wheel of Fortune: What rises will descend, what falls will rise — the wheel turns and you turn with it, wiser each rotation.
  • XI — Justice: The sword cuts cleanly through what pretends; truth has no favourites, only balance.
  • XII — The Hanged Man: Suspended between worlds, he finds the view changes when you surrender the need to stand.
  • XIII — Death: Not an ending but a shedding — the snake leaves its skin and is born sleek and new.
  • XIV — Temperance: Pour one vessel into another; patience is the alchemy that turns lead into gold.
  • XV — The Devil: Chains worn loosely — look closer. You can slip them off whenever you remember you are free.
  • XVI — The Tower: Lightning cracks what was built on lies; from rubble, truth breathes its first honest breath.
  • XVII — The Star: Kneeling by still water, she pours hope back into a thirsty world. Healing begins here.
  • XVIII — The Moon: Shadows shift and nothing is as it seems — trust the path even when you cannot see the ground.
  • XIX — The Sun: Golden and unguarded, joy with nothing to prove — the child dances because the music is enough.
  • XX — Judgement: The trumpet calls and old selves rise; forgive them, gather them, become all you have ever been.
  • XXI — The World: The circle closes, the dancer spins complete — you have arrived, and the journey begins again.

Why Poetry Is a Powerful Way to Learn Tarot

Traditional tarot study often begins with memorizing keywords and reversed meanings. While that approach has value, it can leave the cards feeling flat — like vocabulary lists rather than living archetypes. Poetry engages your intuition directly. When you write or read a poetic line about a card, you bypass the analytical mind and drop into the felt, symbolic layer where tarot actually speaks.

Try this: pull a card from your deck, sit with the image for a full minute, then write two lines — not about what the card means, but about what it feels like. Don't edit. Let the words be strange or imperfect. Over time, you'll build a personal poetic vocabulary for your deck that no guidebook can replicate. This creative approach strengthens your intuitive muscle and makes readings richer and more personal.

  • Write freely — Let images and metaphors arise without censoring. Tarot and poetry both thrive on symbol.
  • Read your poems aloud — Hearing the words activates a different kind of knowing than silent reading.
  • Keep a tarot poetry journal — Over weeks and months, patterns emerge that reveal your unique relationship to the cards.
  • Share with community — Reading circles and creative tarot groups deepen understanding through collective insight.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the 22 Major Arcana cards?

The 22 Major Arcana are the trump cards of a tarot deck, numbered 0 through 21. They represent major life themes and spiritual lessons — from The Fool's innocent beginning through archetypes like The Lovers, Death (transformation), and The World (completion). Together they form "The Fool's Journey," a map of the soul's evolution.

Can poetry really help me learn tarot?

Yes. Poetry accesses the intuitive, symbolic mind — the same faculty you use during readings. Writing short verses for each card creates emotional anchors that are easier to recall than memorized definitions. Many professional readers use creative journaling as a core part of their practice.

Do I need to memorize all 22 cards before getting a reading?

Not at all. A professional tarot reader interprets the cards for you. However, having a basic familiarity with the Major Arcana can deepen your experience and help you engage with the insights more meaningfully. Our readers at Utopia always explain the cards during your session.
